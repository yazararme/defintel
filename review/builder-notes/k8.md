## Schedule impact (measured durations from K7 notes)

Cron schedules only run on main, so the group that matters in production is `publish-refs/heads/main`. The rules:
- A run waits only when another collect-news or pull-drive run on the same ref is already running.
- The ref is part of the group name, so the orchestrator's `dry_run` dispatches on `rev21-33` never interact with main.

- **05:01 collect dispatch → 06:16 pull-drive publish.** The collect run ends by 05:14–05:20 (max 19 min). That leaves about 56 min of slack before 06:16. The publish waits only if the collect dispatch starts more than about 55 min late. The same applies to the 06:15–06:55 pull-drive crons (which never fired in the 13–27 Sep window). Expected delay to the morning publish: none.
- **~10:31–11:32 backstop collect vs. hourly pull-drive.** This is the one real overlap window. The observed pull-drive hourly runs land around 11:00–12:00, and the backstop runs up to 14.4 min. When a pull-drive fires while the backstop is running, it now waits up to about 14 min instead of running in parallel. If the backstop fires during a pull-drive run, it waits at most 1.7 min. By then the report was normally published at 06:16, so these late pull-drive runs are no-ops or catch-ups, and the delay costs nothing that matters.
- **Rough overlap rate.** About 3 hourly pull-drive runs a day land at scattered times. The backstop occupies about 7–15 min of the late morning. That gives a pull-drive/backstop collision on some days, roughly 10–25 %, costing a wait of ≤14 min each time.

## GitHub "1 running + 1 pending" per group — cancel risk

A third arrival in the group cancels the **pending** run, whatever `cancel-in-progress` says. The cases:

| Running | Pending | 3rd arrival | Cancelled | Harm | Realistic probability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| collect (≤14.4 min) | pull-drive | pull-drive | older pull-drive | none: the newer pull-drive pulls the same Drive state | needs two pull-drive fires within ≤14 min, while a collect is running. The hourly slots are 60 min apart; only cron bunching (late firing) could do it. Well under 1 %/day |
| pull-drive (≤1.7 min, no-op 0.2–0.5) | collect | pull-drive | **collect** | the collect run is lost. If it is the 05:01 run, the backstop still collects later that day. If it is the backstop, the 05:01 run already collected | needs a collect arrival inside a ≤1.7 min pull-drive run, **and** another pull-drive fire in that same window. pull-drive fires ≥10 min apart (schedules) and has one daily dispatch, so this cannot happen from the schedule alone. Only a manual pull-drive dispatch at that exact moment could cause it. Effectively 0 (≪0.1 %/day) |
| pull-drive | pull-drive | collect | pull-drive | the publish is delayed to the next pull-drive slot (≤1 h). The report is not lost | needs a pull-drive run lasting more than 10 min (notify loop with slow Pages), plus a collect arriving in that window. Not observed. ≪0.1 %/day |
| collect | collect | any | collect | none. Only 2 collect runs a day, ~5.5 h apart | 0 |

Worst realistic case: a pending pull-drive is replaced by a newer pull-drive. That is harmless. The harmful case (a pending collect is cancelled) needs a manual dispatch at the wrong minute.

**K8-1 evidence.** Two back-to-back `dry_run` collect-news dispatches on `rev21-33` make only 1 running + 1 pending, so neither is cancelled. A third dispatch, or any pull-drive dispatch on `rev21-33` while both are queued, would cancel the pending one.

## Residual risk

- **Push race between collect-news and pull-drive: NO.** Runs on one ref are now serialized, so their `pull --rebase` → `push` sequences cannot interleave. A race with `build.yml` is still possible, as it was before K7. build.yml pushes on human pushes to the build paths and is in its own group. K8 does not change this.
- **Duplicate daily issue from collect-news + pull-drive: NO.** Their `uyari.py` steps can no longer run at the same time. `build.yml` and `missing-report.yml` also call `uyari.py` from their own groups. The unlocked check-then-act in `gonder()` can therefore still produce a duplicate or lost update between one of those and a publish run. The window is a few seconds, the likelihood is low, and the risk is unchanged from before K7. The brief keeps those groups separate on purpose.
